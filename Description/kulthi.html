  • Kulthi (Macrotyloma uniflorum), or horse gram, is an extremely hardy pulse known for its medicinal benefits and extreme drought resistance.

  • Kulthi grows in dry tropical climates (20°C–30°C) on shallow gravelly or degraded soils where no other pulse can survive.

  • Kulthi requires very low water (200–300 mm) and is grown purely under rainfed dryland conditions.

  • Sown late in the season (August–September) or summer (Zaid), it matures in 100–120 days.

  • Kulthi is rich in protein, iron, and antioxidants. In Ayurveda, it is prescribed for dissolving kidney stones. Grown widely in Odisha, Karnataka, and Andhra Pradesh.
