  • Onion (Allium cepa) is one of the most widely consumed vegetable crops in India and around the globe. Used as a staple condiment in daily cooking, it belongs to the Amaryllidaceae family and is valued for its characteristic pungency, flavor, and medicinal properties.

  • Onions require mild weather during early vegetative growth (15°C–20°C) and warm, dry weather during bulb development and maturity (25°C–30°C). Long daylight hours accelerate bulb formation and proper skin curing.

  • Onion has a shallow root system and requires frequent light irrigations. On average, it needs 350–550 mm of water. Stop watering 10–15 days before harvesting to allow skin curing and increase storage life.

  • In India, onions are cultivated in three seasons: Kharif (sown May-June, harvested Oct-Nov), Late Kharif (sown Aug-Sept, harvested Jan-Feb), and Rabi (sown Oct-Nov, harvested April-May). Rabi onion accounts for ~60% of total production and offers the longest storage quality.

  • Onions are low in calories and rich in Vitamin C, Vitamin B6, potassium, and quercetin (a bioflavonoid antioxidant). Economically, onions are a critical high-value commercial crop with major production in Maharashtra (Nashik), Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, and Gujarat.
