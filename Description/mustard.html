  • Mustard (Brassica juncea / Rapeseed), known as Sarson, is the chief winter oilseed crop in Northern and Western India. Yellow and brown mustard seeds are crushed to extract pungent mustard oil, an integral cooking oil in Indian kitchens.

  • Mustard is a subtropical cool-season Rabi crop. It thrives in cool dry winter climates with ideal growing temperatures between 15°C and 25°C. Bright sunshine during pod maturity increases oil content.

  • Mustard has low water requirements (250–400 mm), making it suitable for rainfed or semi-irrigated drylands. Two light irrigations (at flowering and siliqua filling) boost yield substantially.

  • Mustard is sown in October–November and harvested in February–March. Its growth duration ranges between 110 and 140 days depending on variety.

  • Mustard oil contains healthy monounsaturated fatty acids (MUFA), omega-3, and glucosinolates. Rajasthan is the largest mustard producing state, followed by Haryana, Madhya Pradesh, and Uttar Pradesh.
