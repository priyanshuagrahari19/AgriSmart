  • Maize (Zea mays), commonly known as corn, is one of the world's third most important cereal crops after wheat and rice. It serves as food for humans, feed for livestock, and raw material for industrial products like starch, ethanol, bio-fuel, and corn oil. Known as the "Queen of Cereals", it has high yield potential.

  • Maize grows best in warm, sunny climates with temperatures between 18°C and 27°C. It requires full sunlight for photosynthesis and grain filling. Frost or prolonged freezing temperatures severely damage young seedlings and mature plants.

  • Maize requires moderate water, needing 500–800 mm of rainfall or irrigation throughout its growth cycle. Critical irrigation stages include tasseling, silking, and grain filling. While it needs good soil moisture, it cannot tolerate waterlogging, which stunts growth and causes root rot.

  • In India, maize is predominantly grown as a Kharif crop sown in June–July with monsoon arrival. However, in southern and eastern states (Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Tamil Nadu), it is also successfully cultivated during Rabi (winter) and Spring seasons due to favorable mild temperatures.

  • Maize is rich in carbohydrates, dietary fiber, vitamin A (yellow maize), B-complex vitamins, and minerals like phosphorus and magnesium. Industrially, it powers poultry feed industries and food processing. Leading producers in India include Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Bihar, and Rajasthan.
