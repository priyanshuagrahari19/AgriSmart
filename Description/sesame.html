  • Sesame (Sesamum indicum), commonly known as Til, is one of the oldest cultivated oilseed crops in the world. Grown for its oil-rich seeds, sesame oil is highly resistant to rancidity due to natural antioxidants like sesamol and sesamolin.

  • Sesame is a tropical and subtropical crop requiring hot temperatures between 25°C and 35°C for optimal growth. High temperatures during flowering encourage capsule formation, while frost or cold winds reduce seed yield.

  • Sesame is extremely drought-resistant and requires low water (300–400 mm). It is sensitive to waterlogging and heavy rains during seed pod development. Well-drained sandy loam soil is essential.

  • In India, sesame is cultivated primarily as a Kharif crop (June-October) in Northern states, while in Southern states it is cultivated during Rabi and Summer seasons under irrigation. The crop matures quickly in 85 to 105 days.

  • Sesame seeds contain 50% high-quality oil and 20% protein. They are rich in calcium, iron, copper, and omega-6 fatty acids. Economically, sesame is a prime export crop for India, with major growing regions in West Bengal, Rajasthan, Gujarat, and Uttar Pradesh.
