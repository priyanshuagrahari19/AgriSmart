  • Ginger (Zingiber officinale) is an indispensable spice and medicinal crop cultivated for its aromatic underground rhizome. India is one of the largest producers and exporters of ginger in the world.

  • Ginger requires a warm, humid climate with optimum temperatures between 19°C and 28°C. It thrives under light partial shade and heavy rainfall during its growing season.

  • Ginger needs high moisture (1500–3000 mm of annual rainfall or frequent irrigation). Waterlogging causes severe rhizome rot disease, so well-drained sandy loam soil rich in organic humus is mandatory.

  • Ginger is planted in April–May with early pre-monsoon showers and harvested in 8 to 9 months (December–January) for fresh ginger or 10 months for dry ginger (Sonth).

  • Ginger is packed with gingerol, essential oils, and anti-inflammatory compounds. It aids digestion, relieves cold symptoms, and boosts immunity. Key producing states include Assam, Kerala, Meghalaya, Orissa, and Karnataka.
