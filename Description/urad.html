  • Urad bean (Vigna mungo), also known as black gram, is an essential pulse crop native to India. Widely used in Indian culinary dishes (dal, idli, dosa batter, papad), it holds a prominent position due to its rich protein and starch composition.

  • Urad requires a warm and humid climate with optimum growth temperatures ranging between 25°C and 35°C. High humidity and warm days accelerate flowering and pod setting.

  • Urad needs moderate rainfall (400–600 mm). Waterlogging is extremely detrimental to crop survival, while dry spells at flowering can be managed with light supplementary irrigation.

  • Urad is grown predominantly as a Kharif crop during the monsoon (June–October). In southern regions and rice fallows, it is also cultivated during Rabi and Summer seasons as a short-duration (70–90 days) catch crop.

  • Urad is rich in plant-based protein (25%), phosphoric acid, calcium, and iron. It enriches farm soil through root nodule nitrogen fixation. India is both the largest producer and consumer, with major production in Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh, and Maharashtra.
