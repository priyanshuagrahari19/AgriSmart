  • Moong bean (Vigna radiata), also known as green gram, is one of the most important pulse crops grown in India. It is a highly nutritious legume cultivated for its edible seeds, which are consumed as whole dal, split dal, or sprouts. Being a leguminous crop, moong plays a vital role in soil health by fixing atmospheric nitrogen into the soil.

  • Moong thrives best in warm tropical and subtropical climates with an ideal temperature range of 25°C to 35°C. It requires warm weather for seed germination and plant development. Extreme cold, frost, or continuous heavy rainfall during flowering and pod development can damage the crop and reduce grain yield.

  • Moong requires low to moderate water, making it suitable for rainfed areas. It needs around 300–400 mm of water during its 60 to 75-day life cycle. Proper drainage is essential as standing water or waterlogging causes root rot and damages crop production.

  • Moong is primarily cultivated during the Kharif season (June to September) with the arrival of monsoon rains. It is also grown as a short-duration Zaid crop (March to May) after harvesting Rabi crops like wheat. Its quick maturation allows farmers to harvest it in just 60 to 70 days.

  • Moong is rich in digestible protein (24%), dietary fiber, iron, potassium, and antioxidants. It is easily digestible, making it ideal for infants and convalescents. Economically, moong provides quick returns to farmers and improves soil fertility for subsequent crops. Major producing states in India include Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, and Karnataka.
