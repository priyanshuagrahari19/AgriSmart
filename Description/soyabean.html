  • Soyabean (Glycine max) is one of the premier oilseed and pulse crops globally, known as the "Miracle Crop". Rich in protein and oil, soyabean is cultivated for human consumption, soy milk, tofu, soybean oil, and high-protein animal feed.

  • Soyabean grows best in warm, humid climates with temperatures between 20°C and 30°C. Cold temperatures delay seed germination, while excessive heat during flowering causes flower drop.

  • Soyabean requires moderate water (450–700 mm). Flowering and pod development are critical stages where moisture stress leads to poor yield. Well-drained fertile loam soils with good organic matter are ideal.

  • In India, soyabean is grown as a major Kharif crop sown in June–July with the onset of monsoon and harvested in October. Its crop duration ranges from 90 to 110 days.

  • Soyabean contains 40% high-quality protein and 20% edible oil. It fixes atmospheric nitrogen, improving soil productivity. Madhya Pradesh (the "Soy State"), Maharashtra, and Rajasthan account for over 85% of India's soyabean output.
